What is mode decomposition of the EM field, and what modes are natural in free space vs. a cavity?	Instead of treating a generic time-dependent field, decompose it into orthogonal modes. In <b>free space</b> the natural modes are transverse plane waves \( \mathbf{E} \propto e^{i(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}-\omega t)} \) with two polarizations and continuous \(\mathbf{k}\) (\(\omega = kc\)). In a <b>cavity</b> the modes are discrete, e.g. \( E_n(z,t) = E_{0n}e^{-i\omega_n t}\sin(k_n z) \); for a 1D cavity of length \(L\): \( k_n = n\pi/L \).	AQO L17 QuantumOptics
Starting from the classical field energy, what is the quantized single-mode Hamiltonian of the EM field, and what analogy does it reveal?	Classical energy: \( H = \tfrac{1}{2}\int\!\big(\epsilon_0 E^2 + \tfrac{1}{\mu_0}B^2\big)\,dV \). Writing a single mode in terms of \(q(t)\) and \(p(t)=\dot q(t)\) gives \( H = \tfrac{1}{2}(p^2 + \omega^2 q^2) \). <b>A single field mode is equivalent to a simple harmonic oscillator of unit mass</b>, with the electric and magnetic fields playing the roles of canonical position and momentum.	AQO L17 QuantumOptics
How is a single mode of the EM field quantized?	Promote \(q,p\) to operators \(\hat q,\hat p\) with the canonical commutator \([\hat q,\hat p]=i\hbar\), giving \( \hat H = \tfrac{1}{2}(\hat p^2 + \omega^2 \hat q^2) \).	AQO L17 QuantumOptics
Define the annihilation and creation operators and give their commutation relation.	\( \hat a = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\hbar\omega}}(\omega\hat q + i\hat p) \), \( \hat a^\dagger = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\hbar\omega}}(\omega\hat q - i\hat p) \), with \( [\hat a,\hat a^\dagger]=1 \). They destroy/create a photon in the given mode.	AQO L17 QuantumOptics
Write the second-quantized single-mode Hamiltonian and define the number operator.	\( \hat H = \hbar\omega\big(\hat a^\dagger\hat a + \tfrac12\big) = \hbar\omega\big(\hat n + \tfrac12\big) \). The number operator \( \hat n = \hat a^\dagger\hat a \) measures the number of photons in the mode.	AQO L17 QuantumOptics
What are Fock (number) states and how do the ladder operators act on them?	Fock states \(|n\rangle\) are eigenstates of \(\hat n\): \( \hat n|n\rangle = n|n\rangle \); they are orthonormal and complete. \( \hat a|n\rangle = \sqrt{n}\,|n-1\rangle \), \( \hat a|0\rangle = 0 \), \( \hat a^\dagger|n\rangle = \sqrt{n+1}\,|n+1\rangle \). All can be built from vacuum: \( |n\rangle = \tfrac{(\hat a^\dagger)^n}{\sqrt{n!}}|0\rangle \). They are also eigenstates of \(\hat H\).	AQO L17 QuantumOptics
Express the electric and magnetic field operators for a single cavity mode.	\( \hat E_x(z,t) = E_0(\hat a + \hat a^\dagger)\sin(kz) \), \( \hat B_y(z,t) = B_0\,\tfrac{1}{i}(\hat a - \hat a^\dagger)\cos(kz) \), where \( E_0 = \sqrt{\hbar\omega/(\epsilon_0 V)} \) is the electric field "per photon" and \(V\) is the effective mode volume.	AQO L17 QuantumOptics
For a Fock state \(|n\rangle\), what are \(\langle \hat E_x\rangle\) and \(\langle \hat E_x^2\rangle\), and what classical picture do they match?	\( \langle n|\hat E_x|n\rangle = 0 \) (since \(\hat a,\hat a^\dagger\) change \(n\) and \(\langle n|n'\rangle=\delta_{nn'}\)), but \( \langle n|\hat E_x^2|n\rangle = 2E_0^2\sin^2(kz)\big(n+\tfrac12\big) \neq 0 \). This matches a classical harmonic oscillator with known amplitude but random phase: \( \langle x\rangle = 0,\ \langle x^2\rangle > 0 \).	AQO L17 QuantumOptics
Using the Heisenberg equation, what is the time dependence of \(\hat a\)?	\( \tfrac{d\hat a}{dt} = \tfrac{i}{\hbar}[\hat H,\hat a] = -i\omega\hat a \), so \( \hat a(t) = \hat a(0)e^{-i\omega t} \) and \( \hat a^\dagger(t) = \hat a^\dagger(0)e^{i\omega t} \).	AQO L17 QuantumOptics
Why can't a simple Hermitian phase operator be defined in quantum optics?	Two caveats: (1) one can never measure an absolute phase, only the relative phase between two things (any phase measurement is effectively an interferometer); (2) one cannot define a simple Hermitian global phase operator without additional complications. So number–phase uncertainty \(\Delta n\,\Delta\phi\) is intuitively right but not rigorously straightforward.	AQO L17 QuantumOptics
Define the quadrature operators and express the field operator in terms of them.	\( \hat X_1 = \tfrac12(\hat a + \hat a^\dagger) \), \( \hat X_2 = \tfrac{1}{2i}(\hat a - \hat a^\dagger) \). They are the dimensionless \(\hat q,\hat p\). The field becomes \( \hat E_x(z,t) = 2E_0[\hat X_1\cos(\omega t) + \hat X_2\sin(\omega t)]\sin(kz) \): two field components 90° out of phase that together carry amplitude and phase information.	AQO L17 QuantumOptics
How are coherent states defined, and why must their eigenvalue be complex?	Coherent states are eigenstates of the annihilation operator: \( \hat a|\alpha\rangle = \alpha|\alpha\rangle \) with \(\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\). Since \(\hat a\) is non-Hermitian, \(\alpha\) can be complex; also \( \langle\alpha|\hat a^\dagger = \alpha^*\langle\alpha| \).	AQO L18 QuantumOptics
Give the Fock-basis expansion of a coherent state \(|\alpha\rangle\).	\( |\alpha\rangle = e^{-\tfrac12|\alpha|^2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{\alpha^n}{\sqrt{n!}}|n\rangle \), normalized so \(\langle\alpha|\alpha\rangle=1\).	AQO L18 QuantumOptics
What is the photon-number distribution of a coherent state, and what are \(\bar n\) and \(\Delta n\)?	The photon number is Poisson-distributed: \( P_n = |\langle n|\alpha\rangle|^2 = e^{-\bar n}\dfrac{\bar n^n}{n!} \). The mean is \( \bar n = |\alpha|^2 \) and the spread is \( \Delta n = \sqrt{\bar n} = \bar n^{1/2} \), so the fractional uncertainty \( \Delta n/\bar n = 1/\sqrt{\bar n} \) decreases for large \(\bar n\).	AQO L18 QuantumOptics
What are the quadrature expectation values of a coherent state, and how do they connect to the classical field?	\( \langle\alpha|\hat X_1|\alpha\rangle = \mathfrak{Re}(\alpha) \), \( \langle\alpha|\hat X_2|\alpha\rangle = \mathfrak{Im}(\alpha) \), giving \( \langle\alpha|\hat E_x|\alpha\rangle = 2E_0[\mathfrak{Re}(\alpha)\cos\omega t + \mathfrak{Im}(\alpha)\sin\omega t]\sin(kz) \): the expectation value of the quantum field follows the classical field (unlike Fock states, which give zero).	AQO L18 QuantumOptics
What is the quadrature uncertainty of a coherent state, and why is this significant?	\( (\Delta\hat X_1)^2_\alpha = \tfrac14 \), independent of \(\alpha\) — equal to the vacuum fluctuations. Unlike Fock states where \((\Delta\hat X_1)^2_n = \tfrac{n}{2}+\tfrac14\) grows with \(n\), coherent-state fluctuations do not grow with amplitude. Fractional uncertainties shrink as \(1/|\alpha|\), so large-\(|\alpha|\) coherent states approach a classical monochromatic field with fixed amplitude and phase.	AQO L18 QuantumOptics
In phase space, how do you picture the vacuum, a coherent state, and a Fock state?	<b>Vacuum:</b> a symmetric blob at the origin. <b>Coherent state:</b> the same blob displaced to \(\alpha = |\alpha|e^{i\Theta}\) — a <i>displaced vacuum state</i> with \(\Delta X_{1,2}\) independent of \(\alpha\); for large \(|\alpha|\), \(\Delta\Theta\) and \(\Delta n/\bar n \to 0\) (classical limit). <b>Fock state:</b> a ring — well-defined photon number (\(\Delta n=0\)) but completely random phase.	AQO L18 QuantumOptics
What is the time evolution of a coherent state?	Coherent states are <b>not</b> eigenstates of \(\hat H\), so evolution is more than a global phase. Using \( |n\rangle(t) = e^{-i\omega(n+1/2)t}|n\rangle \) one finds \( |\alpha\rangle(t) = e^{-i\omega t/2}|\alpha e^{-i\omega t}\rangle \): the state stays coherent but \(\alpha\) rotates in phase space, so \(\langle\hat E_x\rangle \propto \alpha\cos(\omega t)\) for real \(\alpha\).	AQO L18 QuantumOptics
Are coherent states orthogonal? What basis property do they satisfy?	No — \( |\langle\beta|\alpha\rangle|^2 = e^{-|\beta-\alpha|^2} > 0 \), so distinct coherent states overlap (only vanishing as separation grows). They form an <b>overcomplete</b> basis: \( \int |\alpha\rangle\langle\alpha|\,\dfrac{d^2\alpha}{\pi} = \hat{\mathbb 1} \), with the integral over the complex plane.	AQO L18 QuantumOptics
Define the Husimi Q function and state its key properties.	\( Q(\alpha) = \langle\alpha|\hat\rho|\alpha\rangle/\pi \) — the conceptually simplest phase-space representation, valid for mixed states via \(\hat\rho\). It is normalized \( \int Q(\alpha)\,d^2\alpha = 1 \), and non-negative and bounded: \( 0 \le Q(\alpha) \le 1/\pi \). It is a <b>quasi-probability distribution</b> (not a proper one, since coherent states are non-orthogonal). The Wigner function is another example.	AQO L18 QuantumOptics
What does the Husimi Q function look like for coherent states vs. Fock states?	<b>Coherent states:</b> a Gaussian blob identical to the vacuum's but displaced by \(\alpha\) (confirming "displaced vacuum"); for large \(\alpha\) it approaches a classical field with small \(\Delta\Theta\) and small \(\Delta n/\bar n\). <b>Fock states:</b> radially symmetric (a ring) for all \(n\), since they carry no phase information.	AQO L18 QuantumOptics
How are superposition states and multi-mode states written in quantum optics?	The quantum description allows superpositions naturally, e.g. \( \gamma_1|n=0\rangle + \gamma_2|n=1\rangle \). For several modes, label each by an index: a single photon in mode \(\mathbf{k}_1\) is \( |n=1\rangle_{k_1} \). An atom that could emit into two directions gives the entangled state \( \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\big(|n=1\rangle_{k_1} + |n=1\rangle_{k_2}\big) \).	AQO L18 QuantumOptics
List the particle properties of the photon as derived from the quantized plane-wave mode.	From \(\omega = ck\): photons are <b>massless</b>, move at \(c\), with energy \(E=\hbar\omega\). From \(E^2 = m_0^2c^4 + p^2c^2\) with \(m_0=0\): momentum \(p = \hbar k\). <b>Polarization:</b> two linear or two circular; the circular polarizations map onto photon helicities \(\pm1\). Circularly polarized photons carry spin angular momentum \(\pm\hbar\) along propagation; certain modes (e.g. Laguerre–Gaussian) also carry orbital angular momentum.	AQO L18 QuantumOptics
What is the dipole approximation, and what atom–light Hamiltonian does it yield?	For atoms (~Å) and optical wavelengths (~400–1000 nm), the vector potential is essentially uniform over the atom: \( \mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r},t)\approx\mathbf{A}(t) \). With a suitable gauge, the Hamiltonian becomes \( \hat H = \hat H_0 - \hat{\mathbf d}\cdot\mathbf{E}(t) \), where \( \hat{\mathbf d} = -e\hat{\mathbf r} \) is the dipole operator.	AQO L19 QuantumOptics
Define the two-level system and detuning used in quantum optics.	A two-level atom has ground \(|g\rangle\) and excited \(|e\rangle\) states (eigenstates of \(\hat H_0\)) with resonance frequency \(\hbar\omega_0 = E_e - E_g\). A monochromatic laser of frequency \(\omega\) couples them, with <b>detuning</b> \( \Delta = \omega - \omega_0 \). \(\Delta>0\) is blue-detuned, \(\Delta<0\) is red-detuned.	AQO L19 QuantumOptics
What is the dipole matrix element, and which matrix elements of \(\hat H_0\) and \(\hat V_0\) are non-zero?	Writing \(\hat H = \hat H_0 + \hat V_0\cos(\omega t)\) with \(\hat V_0 = -\hat{\mathbf d}\cdot\mathbf{E}_0\): the diagonal elements vanish (\(\langle g|\hat V_0|g\rangle=\langle e|\hat V_0|e\rangle=0\), since \(\hat{\mathbf d}\) changes parity), but the off-diagonal <b>dipole matrix element</b> \( d_{e,g} = \langle e|\hat{\mathbf d}|g\rangle \neq 0 \) characterizes the transition strength.	AQO L19 QuantumOptics
Define the resonant Rabi frequency.	\( \Omega_0 = \dfrac{dE_0}{\hbar} \), where \(d\) is the (projected) dipole matrix element and \(E_0\) the field amplitude. It sets the rate of coherent population transfer between \(|g\rangle\) and \(|e\rangle\).	AQO L19 QuantumOptics
What is the rotating wave approximation (RWA)?	In the equations of motion, \(\cos(\omega t)\) produces terms rotating at \(\omega-\omega_0=\Delta\) and at \(\omega+\omega_0\). Since \(\Delta\ll\omega+\omega_0\), the fast \(\omega+\omega_0\) terms average out and are dropped. This is the RWA, leaving slowly varying terms: \( \dot c_g = i\tfrac{\Omega_0}{2}e^{i\Delta t}c_e \), \( \dot c_e = i\tfrac{\Omega_0}{2}e^{-i\Delta t}c_g \).	AQO L19 QuantumOptics
Write the two-level Rabi Hamiltonian in the rotating frame (RWA).	After eliminating the oscillating \(e^{i\Delta t}\) by defining \(\tilde c_g, \tilde c_e\), \[ \frac{d}{dt}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde c_g\\ \tilde c_e\end{pmatrix} = \frac{i}{2}\begin{pmatrix}-\Delta & \Omega_0\\ \Omega_0 & \Delta\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde c_g\\ \tilde c_e\end{pmatrix}, \] equivalently \( H = \tfrac{\hbar}{2}\begin{pmatrix}-\Delta & \Omega_0\\ \Omega_0 & \Delta\end{pmatrix} \).	AQO L19 QuantumOptics
Describe resonant Rabi oscillations (\(\Delta=0\)) for an atom starting in \(|g\rangle\).	With \(\Delta=0\) and \(\tilde c_g(0)=1\): \( \tilde c_g(t) = \cos(\Omega_0 t/2) \), \( \tilde c_e(t) = i\sin(\Omega_0 t/2) \). The populations oscillate: \( |\tilde c_e(t)|^2 = \sin^2(\Omega_0 t/2) = \tfrac12\{1-\cos(\Omega_0 t)\} \), fully cycling between ground and excited state.	AQO L19 QuantumOptics
Describe detuned Rabi oscillations and define the generalized Rabi frequency.	For \(\Delta\neq0\) the <b>effective (generalized) Rabi frequency</b> is \( \Omega = \sqrt{\Omega_0^2 + \Delta^2} \ge \Omega_0 \). Starting in \(|g\rangle\): \( |\tilde c_e(t)|^2 = \dfrac{\Omega_0^2}{\Omega^2}\sin^2\!\big(\tfrac12\Omega t\big) \). The oscillation is faster but its amplitude \(\Omega_0^2/\Omega^2 \le 1\) is reduced — full transfer is impossible off resonance.	AQO L19 QuantumOptics
Why is spontaneous emission described with density matrices rather than pure states?	An excited atom in vacuum decays as \(|c_e(t)|^2 = e^{-\gamma t}\), emitting a photon into one of many modes. The environment effectively measures the emitted photons, so the pure state \(|\Psi\rangle = \alpha|e\rangle + \beta|g\rangle\) becomes a <b>statistical mixture</b> of \(|e\rangle\) and \(|g\rangle\), which requires a density matrix \( \hat\rho = \begin{pmatrix}\rho_{gg} & \rho_{ge}\\ \rho_{eg} & \rho_{ee}\end{pmatrix} \).	AQO L20 QuantumOptics
In the density matrix, what are populations and coherences?	The diagonal elements \(\rho_{gg},\rho_{ee}\) are the <b>populations</b> (probabilities of being in \(|g\rangle\) or \(|e\rangle\)), with \(\rho_{gg}+\rho_{ee}=1\). The off-diagonal elements \(\rho_{ge},\rho_{eg}\) are the <b>coherences</b>, with \(\rho_{eg}=\rho_{ge}^*\).	AQO L20 QuantumOptics
What are the Optical Bloch Equations and what do the \(\gamma\) terms describe?	The OBEs add spontaneous emission to the density-matrix evolution: \[ \dot\rho_{ee} = -\gamma\rho_{ee} + i\tfrac{\Omega_0}{2}(\rho_{ge}-\rho_{eg}), \] \[ \dot\rho_{ge} = -(\gamma/2 + i\Delta)\rho_{ge} + i\tfrac{\Omega_0}{2}(\rho_{ee}-\rho_{gg}). \] The \(\pm\gamma\rho_{ee}\) terms describe decay of the excited-state population; the \(-\gamma/2\) in the off-diagonals describes decay of coherence due to spontaneous emission. Trace is preserved: \(\rho_{gg}+\rho_{ee}=1\).	AQO L20 QuantumOptics
Define the population inversion and give the reduced OBEs.	The <b>population inversion</b> is \( w = \rho_{ee} - \rho_{gg} \) (\(w=+1\): fully excited; \(w=-1\): fully ground). The four OBEs reduce to two: \[ \dot\rho_{eg} = -(\gamma/2 - i\Delta)\rho_{eg} - i\tfrac{\Omega_0}{2}w, \] \[ \dot w = -\gamma(w+1) - i\Omega_0(\rho_{eg}-\rho_{ge}). \]	AQO L20 QuantumOptics
Define the saturation parameter \(S\) and resonant saturation \(S_0\).	\( S = \dfrac{\Omega_0^2/2}{\Delta^2 + \gamma^2/4} = \dfrac{S_0}{1 + 4\Delta^2/\gamma^2} \), with resonant value \( S_0 = \dfrac{2\Omega_0^2}{\gamma^2} \). Since \(S_0 \propto \Omega_0^2 \propto E_0^2 \propto I\), it is often written \( S_0 = I/I_{\text{sat}} \), where \(I_{\text{sat}}\propto\gamma^2\) depends on the transition.	AQO L20 QuantumOptics
What is the steady-state population inversion of a driven two-level atom?	\( w_{\text{st}} = -\dfrac{1}{1+S} \). For any \(\gamma>0\) the system reaches a steady state (\(\dot\rho_{eg}=\dot w=0\)); the inversion saturates toward \(w=0\) (equal populations) for large \(S\), but never goes positive.	AQO L20 QuantumOptics
Give the steady-state fluorescence (scattering) rate and its three intensity regimes.	\( \Gamma_{\text{sc}} = \gamma\rho_{ee} = \dfrac{\gamma}{2}\dfrac{S}{1+S} \). Regimes: (1) \(I\ll I_{\text{sat}}\): \(\Gamma_{\text{sc}}\propto I\) (linear, constant cross-section); (2) \(I\approx I_{\text{sat}}\): the transition begins to saturate; (3) \(I\gg I_{\text{sat}}\): fully saturated, \(\Gamma_{\text{sc}}\to\Gamma^{\max}_{\text{sc}}=\gamma/2\).	AQO L20 QuantumOptics
What is power broadening, and what is the natural linewidth?	At low intensity (\(S_0\ll1\)) the fluorescence vs. detuning is a Lorentzian of width \(\gamma\) — the <b>natural linewidth</b>. At higher intensity the lineshape stays Lorentzian but with increased width \( \gamma' = \gamma\sqrt{1+S_0} \). This intensity-dependent broadening is called <b>power (saturation) broadening</b>.	AQO L20 QuantumOptics
Define the Bloch vector and write the Bloch equations in vector form.	The Bloch vector \((u,v,w)\): \( u = \rho_{ge}+\rho_{eg} = 2\,\mathfrak{Re}(\rho_{ge}) \), \( v = i(\rho_{ge}-\rho_{eg}) = 2\,\mathfrak{Im}(\rho_{ge}) \), \( w = \rho_{ee}-\rho_{gg} \). The Bloch equations become \[ \frac{d}{dt}\begin{pmatrix}u\\v\\w\end{pmatrix} = -\begin{pmatrix}\Omega_0\\0\\\Delta\end{pmatrix}\times\begin{pmatrix}u\\v\\w\end{pmatrix} - \gamma\begin{pmatrix}u/2\\v/2\\w+1\end{pmatrix}. \]	AQO L20 QuantumOptics
What are the longitudinal and transverse relaxation times \(T_1\) and \(T_2\)?	The longitudinal decay rate \(\gamma_\parallel\) governs population decay, with \( T_1 = 1/\gamma_\parallel \). The transverse decay rate \(\gamma_\perp \ge \gamma_\parallel/2\) governs decay of coherences and can include extra dephasing beyond that from \(\gamma_\parallel\); it gives the dephasing time \( T_2 = 1/\gamma_\perp \).	AQO L20 QuantumOptics
What is the Bloch sphere, and how does the state evolve on it without dissipation?	Ignoring dissipation, the Bloch equations are \( \tfrac{d}{dt}(u,v,w) = -(\Omega_0,0,\Delta)\times(u,v,w) \): the Bloch vector <b>precesses</b> about the axis \((-\Omega_0,0,-\Delta)\) at the generalized Rabi frequency \(\Omega=\sqrt{\Omega_0^2+\Delta^2}\). All pure states satisfy \( u^2+v^2+w^2=1 \) — the surface of the Bloch sphere. Mixed states lie inside (\(u^2+v^2+w^2<1\)), as produced by spontaneous emission.	AQO L20 QuantumOptics
What do \(\pi/2\)- and \(\pi\)-pulses do on the Bloch sphere?	On resonance the rotation axis is \((-\Omega_0,0,0)\) (precession about the \(u\)-axis). A <b>\(\pi/2\)-pulse</b> (\(\Omega_0 t = \pi/2\)) creates an equal superposition of ground and excited state; a <b>\(\pi\)-pulse</b> (\(\Omega_0 t = \pi\)) swaps ground and excited state. A pulse area of \(3\pi/2\) gives an equal superposition with opposite phase. Detuning shifts the rotation axis off the \(u\)-axis — this sensitivity underlies atomic clocks.	AQO L20 QuantumOptics
What are quantum jumps, and what system reveals them?	In a three-level V-system, \(|1\rangle\!\to\!|0\rangle\) is a strong, saturated transition while \(|2\rangle\) is metastable (weak \(|2\rangle\!\to\!|0\rangle\)). Strong fluorescence on \(|1\rangle\!\to\!|0\rangle\) acts as a continuous measurement of whether the atom is "bright" (\(|0\rangle\)) or "dark" (\(|2\rangle\)). This measurement disrupts the coherent \(|0\rangle\leftrightarrow|2\rangle\) Rabi oscillation, turning it into a random telegraph signal: the atom abruptly "jumps" between bright (fluorescing) and dark periods.	AQO L20 QuantumOptics
What are dressed states and bare states?	Diagonalizing the two-level Hamiltonian (with energy offset) \( \hat H = \hbar\begin{pmatrix}0 & \Omega_0/2\\ \Omega_0/2 & -\Delta\end{pmatrix} \) gives the <b>dressed states</b> \(|+\rangle,|-\rangle\) — the eigenstates in the presence of the field. The atomic eigenstates \(|g\rangle,|e\rangle\) without the field are the <b>bare states</b>. Weak detuned fields only slightly mix the bare states — they "dress" them.	AQO L21 QuantumOptics
Give the dressed-state energies and eigenstates.	Energies: \( E_\pm/\hbar = -\dfrac{\Delta}{2} \pm \dfrac{\Omega}{2} \), with \( \Omega = \sqrt{\Omega_0^2+\Delta^2} \). Eigenstates: \( |+\rangle = \sin\Theta\,|g\rangle + \cos\Theta\,|e\rangle \), \( |-\rangle = \cos\Theta\,|g\rangle - \sin\Theta\,|e\rangle \), where \( \tan(2\Theta) = -\Omega_0/\Delta \). The splitting is \(\Omega\), minimized to \(\Omega_0\) on resonance.	AQO L21 QuantumOptics
What is the AC Stark (light) shift, and how does it lead to dipole traps?	In the far red-detuned regime (\(\Delta<0\), \(|\Delta|\gg\Omega_0\)), \(|-\rangle\approx|g\rangle\) and its energy shifts by \( \Delta E = -\dfrac{\hbar}{4}\dfrac{\Omega_0^2}{|\Delta|} \propto -\dfrac{I}{|\Delta|} \). So red-detuned light <b>attracts</b> ground-state atoms to high intensity. Since the scattering rate \( \Gamma_{\text{sc}}\propto I/\Delta^2 \) falls faster with \(\Delta\), at large detuning atoms feel a conservative potential \(V\propto I\) with negligible scattering — the basis of <b>dipole traps, optical tweezers, and optical lattices</b>.	AQO L21 QuantumOptics
What is rapid adiabatic passage (RAP)?	The dressed-state composition changes continuously with detuning. If the field is switched on at large negative \(\Delta\), then \(|-\rangle\) coincides with \(|g\rangle\); slowly ramping \(\Delta\) from large negative to large positive values makes the atom adiabatically follow \(|-\rangle\), which ends up as \(|e\rangle\). This gives robust population inversion.	AQO L21 QuantumOptics
What is the Jaynes–Cummings model Hamiltonian?	A two-level atom coupled to a single quantized cavity mode. After the dipole approximation and RWA (dropping \(\sigma a\) and \(\sigma^\dagger a^\dagger\)): \[ H_{JC} = \hbar\omega_0\,\sigma^\dagger\sigma + \hbar\omega\,\hat a^\dagger\hat a + \hbar g(\sigma^\dagger a + \sigma a^\dagger), \] where \(\sigma=|g\rangle\langle e|\) is the atomic lowering operator and \(g\) is the cavity-QED coupling constant.	AQO L21 QuantumOptics
Define the cavity-QED coupling constant \(g_0\) and explain what controls it.	\( g_0 = -\boldsymbol\epsilon\cdot\mathbf d_{e,g}\sqrt{\dfrac{\omega}{2\epsilon_0\hbar V}} \), with \(g(\mathbf r) = g_0 f'(\mathbf r)\). It depends on the dipole matrix element and the field strength at the atom. <b>Small mode volume \(V\)</b> concentrates the field and gives stronger coupling.	AQO L21 QuantumOptics
What is the natural basis of the Jaynes–Cummings model, and what dynamics couple the states?	The basis is atom \(\otimes\) field Fock states: \( |g,n\rangle = |g\rangle\otimes|n\rangle \), with energies forming a "tower" structure. The interaction couples only \(|e,n\rangle \leftrightarrow |g,n+1\rangle\) — these are <b>closed</b> two-level pairs, undergoing Rabi oscillations at \( \Omega_0 = 2g\sqrt{n+1} \), generalized \( \Omega = \sqrt{4(n+1)g^2 + \Delta^2} \).	AQO L21 QuantumOptics
What are quantum (vacuum) Rabi oscillations and why do they dephase in a coherent field?	An excited atom in an empty cavity still oscillates with \(|g,1\rangle\) — the \(n=0\) Rabi frequency \(\Omega_0=2g\) (vacuum Rabi oscillation). If the cavity holds a coherent field, it is a superposition of many \(|n\rangle\) so the dynamics contains many Rabi frequencies \(\propto\sqrt{n+1}\), which <b>dephase</b> (and later revive).	AQO L21 QuantumOptics
What is the Purcell effect, and what is the strong-coupling regime of cavity QED?	<b>Purcell effect:</b> the cavity modifies the density of field modes, so the atom's spontaneous emission rate \(\gamma\) differs from free space; once \(g\gg\gamma\), emission goes dominantly into the cavity mode. Photons also leak out at rate \(\kappa\). The Jaynes–Cummings model describes the <b>strong-coupling regime</b>, where \( g \gg \kappa,\gamma \).	AQO L21 QuantumOptics
Define the second-order coherence \(g^{(2)}(\tau)\) for quantum and classical fields.	\( g^{(2)}(\tau) = \dfrac{\langle E^{(-)}(t)E^{(-)}(t+\tau)E^{(+)}(t+\tau)E^{(+)}(t)\rangle}{\langle E^{(-)}(t)E^{(+)}(t)\rangle^2} \). For classical fields it becomes an intensity correlation: \( g^{(2)}_{\text{cl}}(\tau) = \dfrac{\langle I(t)I(t+\tau)\rangle}{\langle I\rangle^2} \).	AQO L21 QuantumOptics
What constraint applies to \(g^{(2)}(0)\) for classical light, and what is photon bunching?	For classical light, \(\langle(I-\langle I\rangle)^2\rangle\ge0\) implies \(\langle I^2\rangle\ge\langle I\rangle^2\), so \( g^{(2)}_{\text{cl}}(0) = \langle I^2\rangle/\langle I\rangle^2 \ge 1 \). A monochromatic wave gives \(g^{(2)}=1\); <b>thermal light</b> is spectrally broad and shows <b>photon bunching</b> — \(g^{(2)}(0)>1\), photons arrive preferentially in bunches.	AQO L21 QuantumOptics
What is photon antibunching, and what does it reveal?	Light from a <b>single emitter</b> (single atom, quantum dot, NV/colour centre) has \( g^{(2)}(0) = 0 \), because a single emitter can emit only one photon at a time. This is <b>antibunching</b> — impossible for classical light (which requires \(g^{(2)}(0)\ge1\)) — and is the signature of a true single-photon source.	AQO L21 QuantumOptics
What is the Hanbury-Brown & Twiss experiment?	An intensity (\(g^{(2)}\)) correlation measurement. Historically (HB&T 1958, "intensity interferometer" on Sirius A) the correlations were measured spatially as \(g^{(2)}(r)\); the decay of these correlations let HB&T deduce the angular size of the star. Modern HB&T setups use a beam splitter and two detectors to measure \(g^{(2)}(\tau)\) and classify light as bunched, coherent, or antibunched.	AQO L21 QuantumOptics
What are single-photon detectors (SPDs), and what are their main limitations?	SPDs produce a detectable electrical signal from a single absorbed photon, e.g. avalanche photomultipliers / SPADs, or superconducting nanowire detectors (SNSPDs, photon heats the wire out of the superconducting state). They can reach high quantum efficiency \(\eta > 90\text{–}98\%\) but suffer from <b>dark counts</b> (false events) and <b>dead time</b> (cannot register a second photon immediately). Photon-number-resolving (PNR) detectors can count photons in small pulses.	AQO L22 QuantumOptics
Give the quantum beam-splitter mode transformation and the reciprocity relations.	For input modes \(\hat a_0,\hat a_1\) and outputs \(\hat a_2,\hat a_3\): \( \begin{pmatrix}\hat a_2\\\hat a_3\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}t' & r\\ r' & t\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\hat a_0\\\hat a_1\end{pmatrix} \). Preserving the commutators for all four modes requires <b>reciprocity relations</b>: \( |r'|=|r| \), \( |t'|=|t| \), \( |r|^2+|t|^2=1 \), \( r^*t'+r't^*=0 \), \( r^*t+r't'^*=0 \).	AQO L22 QuantumOptics
For a 50:50 beam splitter, give the standard output mode operators and explain why the empty port matters.	With a phase shift on reflection: \( \hat a_2 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(\hat a_0 + i\hat a_1) \), \( \hat a_3 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(i\hat a_0 + \hat a_1) \). It is <b>impossible</b> to write consistent relations without including the unused input mode \(\hat a_0\): even if no light enters that port, its <b>vacuum fluctuations affect the output</b>.	AQO L22 QuantumOptics
What happens when a single photon hits a 50:50 beam splitter?	Using \( \hat a_1^\dagger = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(i\hat a_2^\dagger + \hat a_3^\dagger) \), one photon in mode 1 becomes \( |\text{out}\rangle = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\big(i|1\rangle_2|0\rangle_3 + |0\rangle_2|1\rangle_3\big) \) — an <b>entangled</b> state. A photon-number measurement gives 50:50 detection, but interfering the modes on a second beam splitter shows the photon took both paths. A coherent state, by contrast, splits into two coherent states with no entanglement: \( |0\rangle_0|\alpha\rangle_1 \to |i\alpha/\sqrt2\rangle_2\,|\alpha/\sqrt2\rangle_3 \).	AQO L22 QuantumOptics
What happens when two indistinguishable photons enter a beam splitter, one per port?	The input \( |1\rangle_0|1\rangle_1 = \hat a_0^\dagger\hat a_1^\dagger|0\rangle \) transforms to \( \tfrac{i}{\sqrt2}\big(|2\rangle_2|0\rangle_3 + |0\rangle_2|2\rangle_3\big) \). The cross term cancels by interference: <b>the two photons always exit together</b> at the same output port — they never split. This is the basis of the Hong–Ou–Mandel effect.	AQO L22 QuantumOptics
Define photon indistinguishability and how it is quantified.	A realistic photon is a finite-length pulse — a superposition of many frequencies, described by \( \hat a_{\text{pulse}} = \sum_s\int c(\omega,s)\hat a_{\omega,s}\,d\omega \). Photons in the <b>same</b> mode are indistinguishable; photons in <b>orthogonal</b> modes are distinguishable. The indistinguishability is \( I = |\langle\psi_1|\psi_2\rangle|^2 \).	AQO L22 QuantumOptics
What is the Hong–Ou–Mandel dip?	Two identical photons entering a beam splitter (one per port) always leave together, so the <b>coincidence count</b> (one photon at each output) drops to zero. Introducing a relative delay \(\tau\) makes the photons distinguishable and restores coincidences, so the coincidence rate shows a characteristic <b>dip at \(\tau=0\)</b>. The depth of the dip measures photon indistinguishability.	AQO L22 QuantumOptics
What is a \(\Lambda\)-system, and what is its RWA Hamiltonian?	A three-level system with two ground/metastable states (\(|1\rangle,|3\rangle\)) coupled to a common excited state \(|2\rangle\) by a pump field (\(\Omega_p\)) and a Stokes/control field (\(\Omega_s\)). In the RWA: \[ \hat H = \frac{\hbar}{2}\begin{pmatrix}0 & \Omega_p & 0\\ \Omega_p & 2\Delta_p & \Omega_s\\ 0 & \Omega_s & 2(\Delta_p-\Delta_s)\end{pmatrix}. \] The <b>two-photon (Raman) detuning</b> is \( \delta = \Delta_p - \Delta_s \).	AQO L22 QuantumOptics
What is a dark state in a \(\Lambda\)-system?	At two-photon resonance (\(\delta=0\)), one dressed state is the <b>dark state</b> \( |d^0\rangle = \cos\theta\,|1\rangle - \sin\theta\,|3\rangle \), with \( \tan\theta = \Omega_p/\Omega_s \). It contains <b>no admixture of the excited state \(|2\rangle\)</b>, so it emits no fluorescence even if \(|2\rangle\) can spontaneously decay — it is "dark".	AQO L22 QuantumOptics
What is STIRAP and what makes it robust?	<b>Stimulated Raman Adiabatic Passage:</b> the dark state \( |d^0\rangle = \cos\theta|1\rangle - \sin\theta|3\rangle \) continuously changes from \(|1\rangle\) to \(|3\rangle\) as \(\theta\) varies, so adiabatically following it transfers population \(|1\rangle\to|3\rangle\). It uses a <b>counterintuitive pulse order</b> (Stokes before pump), needs Raman resonance (\(\delta\ll\Omega_p,\Omega_s\)), and the \(\theta\) change must be slow on the scale of \(1/\Omega_{p,s}\). Because the transfer never populates the lossy \(|2\rangle\), STIRAP is fast (for large \(\Omega_{p,s}\)), very robust, and widely used.	AQO L22 QuantumOptics
